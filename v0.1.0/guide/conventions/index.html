Conventions

SMLMCore defines the types that the JuliaSMLM packages share, and with them the conventions on this page: units, pixel coordinates, array order, ROIs, sCMOS calibration, numbering, and emitter uncertainties.

Units

QuantityUnit
positions x, y, z; pixel sizes and pixel edgesµm
position standard deviations σ_x, σ_y, σ_zµm
position covariances σ_xy, σ_xz, σ_yzµm²
photons of an Emitter2D or Emitter3Dphotons
fitted photons (total in the frame) and σ_photonsthe fitter's working units: photons, or photoelectrons for sCMOS data (below)
fitted bg and σ_bgthe same units, per pixel
sCMOS offsetADU
sCMOS gaine⁻/ADU
sCMOS readnoisee⁻ rms
sCMOS qe0 to 1
ROI datathe camera's raw units (ADU for an SCMOSCamera)
ROI sizes and corners, pixel indicespixels

Typical sCMOS values: offset 100 to 500 ADU, gain 0.1 to 2 e⁻/ADU depending on the readout mode, read noise 0.3 to 5 e⁻ rms, QE 0.5 to 0.95.

The photons of an Emitter2D or Emitter3D are the photons from the emitter that reach the camera in the frame, before QE.

In the fit types (Emitter2DFit, Emitter3DFit), fitted photons (the total in the frame) and bg (per pixel) are in the units the fitter works in: photons when the data are in photons (an IdealCamera, or an SCMOSCamera with qe = 1), and photoelectrons (photons × qe) when the fitter converts SCMOSCamera ADU with (ADU - offset) * gain, as GaussMLE does. A simulator that fills these types with ground truth, as SMLMSim does, stores photons before QE, as in Emitter2D.

pixel_size is the size of a pixel at the sample: the sensor pixel divided by the magnification. A 6.5 µm sensor pixel behind a 100× objective is 0.065 µm.

Pixels and coordinates

Pixels are labeled (x, y) = (col, row); arrays are indexed [row, col].

  • The origin, (0, 0) µm, is the top-left corner of the camera. x increases to the right, along the columns; y increases downward, along the rows.
  • Columns and rows are numbered from 1, so pixel (1, 1) is the top-left pixel.
  • A camera stores its pixel edges, pixel_edges_x and pixel_edges_y: N + 1 edges per axis for N pixels, increasing from 0. Constructors do not check this; physical_to_pixel* and the uniform-pixel formulas assume it, and the cameras' displays assume uniform pixels (they report the first pixel's width).
  • With uniform pixels of Δx × Δy, pixel (col, row) covers [(col - 1)·Δx, col·Δx) × [(row - 1)·Δy, row·Δy) µm and its center is at ((col - 0.5)·Δx, (row - 0.5)·Δy) µm. In pixel coordinates the center of pixel (col, row) is (col, row), and the camera's corner is (0.5, 0.5).
  • A position on a pixel edge belongs to the pixel that starts there, so (0, 0) µm is in pixel (1, 1) (physical_to_pixel_index). This holds up to floating-point rounding: a position computed as (n - 1) * pixel_size can fall in pixel n - 1.
  • pixel_to_physical, physical_to_pixel and physical_to_pixel_index take one scalar pixel size: they hold for square pixels with the edges starting at 0. For rectangular pixels or a camera with its own edges, use get_pixel_centers and the camera's edges. None of them checks that a position lies inside the camera.

Array order

Images and every per-pixel array are indexed [row, col] = [y, x], the order of a camera frame in Julia:

  • An sCMOS calibration map has size (ny, nx), and map[row, col] belongs to pixel (x, y) = (col, row). A map of the wrong size throws a DimensionMismatch, but a square sensor's map cannot be checked for orientation: a transposed map is accepted silently.
  • ROI data are indexed [row, col] (SingleROI) and [row, col, roi] (ROIBatch).

ROIs

  • An ROI is a square of roi_size × roi_size pixels cut from one camera frame.
  • Its corner is the camera pixel (x, y) = (col, row) of its top-left pixel, numbered from 1: corner in a SingleROI, x_corners[k] and y_corners[k] in an ROIBatch.
  • Inside the ROI, [1, 1] is the top-left pixel. ROI pixel data[row, col, k] is camera pixel (x, y) = (x_corners[k] + col - 1, y_corners[k] + row - 1). For uniform pixels of Δx × Δy its center is at ((x_corners[k] + col - 1.5)·Δx, (y_corners[k] + row - 1.5)·Δy) µm.
  • ROI data are in the camera's raw units: ADU for an SCMOSCamera, which a fitter converts to electrons with the offset and gain of the camera pixels under the ROI. That conversion relies on the map order above and on the corner convention.

sCMOS calibration

An SCMOSCamera describes how the photons that reach a pixel become its reading:

photons reaching the pixel   N
photoelectrons               n ~ Poisson(N × qe)
read noise (electrons)       ε ~ Normal(0, readnoise²)
reading (ADU)                (n + ε) / gain + offset

Each of offset, gain, readnoise and qe is a scalar, the same for every pixel, or a map of size (ny, nx). Spec-sheet values give scalars; per-pixel calibration gives maps. The defaults, offset = 0, gain = 1 and qe = 1, describe data already in photons. A scalar and a uniform map of the same value make different cameras: cat_smld and merge_smld refuse to combine them.

Frames, datasets and tracks

  • Frame and dataset numbers start at 1. An SMLD's n_frames and n_datasets count them.
  • In the fit types, track_id = 0 means the localization is not linked into a track, and id = 0 that no identifier is set.

Emitter uncertainties

σ_x, σ_y and σ_z are standard deviations (µm), and σ_xy, σ_xz and σ_yz are covariances (µm²). The position covariance of an Emitter2DFit is

\[\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_x^2 & \sigma_{xy} \\ \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_y^2 \end{pmatrix},\]

and of an Emitter3DFit

\[\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_x^2 & \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_{xz} \\ \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_y^2 & \sigma_{yz} \\ \sigma_{xz} & \sigma_{yz} & \sigma_z^2 \end{pmatrix}.\]

A covariance of 0 means the uncertainty ellipse (or ellipsoid) is aligned with the axes.
