The package

SMLMCore — Module
SMLMCore

Types for single molecule localization microscopy (SMLM) data: emitters (localizations), cameras, ROI batches and SMLD containers, with coordinate conversions, filters, and functions that combine data sets. Lengths are in µm.

Example

julia> cam = IdealCamera(512, 512, 0.1);

julia> emitters = [
           Emitter2DFit{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 1000.0, 10.0, 0.01, 0.01, 50.0, 2.0),
           Emitter2DFit{Float64}(3.0, 4.0, 1200.0, 12.0, 0.01, 0.01, 60.0, 2.0),
       ];

julia> smld = BasicSMLD(emitters, cam, 1, 1);

julia> length(@filter(smld, photons > 1100)), length(filter_roi(smld, (0.0, 2.0), (1.0, 3.0)))
(1, 1)

Emitters

SMLMCore.AbstractEmitter — Type
AbstractEmitter

Supertype of all emitter (localization) types. Positions are in µm.

A custom emitter type subtypes AbstractEmitter and has the properties x and y (and z for 3D) in µm and photons. filter_frames needs a frame property. In merge_smld, adjust_frames needs frame and adjust_datasets needs dataset. Declare the type's dimension as emitter_ndims describes.

SMLMCore.Emitter2D — Type
Emitter2D{T} <: AbstractEmitter

A 2D emitter with a position and a photon count, as used for simulation and ground truth.

Fields

  • x::T: x position (µm)
  • y::T: y position (µm)
  • photons::T: number of photons from the emitter that reach the camera in the frame, before QE

It has no frame or dataset field, so filter_frames and renumbering in merge_smld do not apply to it.

Constructors

  • Emitter2D{T}(x, y, photons), or Emitter2D(x, y, photons) with all three of one type.

SMLMCore.Emitter2DFit — Type
Emitter2DFit{T} <: AbstractEmitter

A fitted 2D localization: position, photons and background with their uncertainties, and the frame, dataset and track it belongs to.

Fields

  • x::T, y::T: fitted position (µm)
  • photons::T: fitted signal, the total in the frame, in the fitter's working units (below)
  • bg::T: fitted background per pixel, in the same units
  • σ_x::T, σ_y::T: standard deviations of the position (µm)
  • σ_xy::T: covariance of x and y (µm²); 0 when the uncertainty ellipse is axis-aligned
  • σ_photons::T: standard deviation of photons, in its units
  • σ_bg::T: standard deviation of bg, in its units
  • frame::Int: frame number, 1-based
  • dataset::Int: dataset number, 1-based
  • track_id::Int: track the localization is linked into (0 = unlinked)
  • id::Int: identifier within the dataset (0 = not set)

photons (the total in the frame) and bg (per pixel) are in the units the fitter works in: photons when the data are in photons (an IdealCamera, or an SCMOSCamera with qe = 1), and photoelectrons (photons × qe) when the fitter converts SCMOSCamera ADU with (ADU - offset) * gain, as GaussMLE does. A simulator that fills these types with ground truth, as SMLMSim does, stores photons before QE: the photons that reach the camera in the frame, as in Emitter2D.

The position covariance is $\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_x^2 & \sigma_{xy} \\ \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_y^2 \end{pmatrix}$: σ_x and σ_y are standard deviations (µm), and σ_xy is a covariance (µm²).

Constructors

  • Emitter2DFit{T}(x, y, photons, bg, σ_x, σ_y, σ_photons, σ_bg; σ_xy=zero(T), frame=1, dataset=1, track_id=0, id=0): the eight values must already be of type T (write 1000.0, not 1000).
  • Emitter2DFit{T}(x, y, photons, bg, σ_x, σ_y, σ_xy, σ_photons, σ_bg, frame, dataset, track_id, id): every field, in field order.

Example

julia> e = Emitter2DFit{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 1000.0, 10.0, 0.01, 0.01, 50.0, 2.0; frame=5);

julia> (e.x, e.y, e.frame, e.dataset, e.σ_xy)
(1.0, 2.0, 5, 1, 0.0)

SMLMCore.Emitter3DFit — Type
Emitter3DFit{T} <: AbstractEmitter

A fitted 3D localization: position, photons and background with their uncertainties, and the frame, dataset and track it belongs to.

Fields

  • x::T, y::T, z::T: fitted position (µm)
  • photons::T: fitted signal, the total in the frame, in the fitter's working units (below)
  • bg::T: fitted background per pixel, in the same units
  • σ_x::T, σ_y::T, σ_z::T: standard deviations of the position (µm)
  • σ_xy::T, σ_xz::T, σ_yz::T: covariances of the position (µm²); 0 = uncorrelated
  • σ_photons::T: standard deviation of photons, in its units
  • σ_bg::T: standard deviation of bg, in its units
  • frame::Int: frame number, 1-based
  • dataset::Int: dataset number, 1-based
  • track_id::Int: track the localization is linked into (0 = unlinked)
  • id::Int: identifier within the dataset (0 = not set)

photons (the total in the frame) and bg (per pixel) are in the units the fitter works in: photons when the data are in photons (an IdealCamera, or an SCMOSCamera with qe = 1), and photoelectrons (photons × qe) when the fitter converts SCMOSCamera ADU with (ADU - offset) * gain, as GaussMLE does. A simulator that fills these types with ground truth, as SMLMSim does, stores photons before QE: the photons that reach the camera in the frame, as in Emitter2D.

The position covariance is $\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_x^2 & \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_{xz} \\ \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_y^2 & \sigma_{yz} \\ \sigma_{xz} & \sigma_{yz} & \sigma_z^2 \end{pmatrix}$: σ_x, σ_y and σ_z are standard deviations (µm), and σ_xy, σ_xz and σ_yz are covariances (µm²).

Constructors

  • Emitter3DFit{T}(x, y, z, photons, bg, σ_x, σ_y, σ_z, σ_photons, σ_bg; σ_xy=zero(T), σ_xz=zero(T), σ_yz=zero(T), frame=1, dataset=1, track_id=0, id=0): the ten values must already be of type T.
  • Emitter3DFit{T}(x, y, z, photons, bg, σ_x, σ_y, σ_z, σ_xy, σ_xz, σ_yz, σ_photons, σ_bg, frame, dataset, track_id, id): every field, in field order.

Example

julia> e = Emitter3DFit{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, -0.5, 1000.0, 10.0, 0.01, 0.01, 0.02, 50.0, 2.0;
                                 σ_xz=0.002, frame=5);

julia> (e.z, e.σ_z, e.σ_xz, e.frame)
(-0.5, 0.02, 0.002, 5)

SMLMCore.emitter_ndims — Function
emitter_ndims(smld::AbstractSMLD)
emitter_ndims(emitters::AbstractVector{<:AbstractEmitter})
emitter_ndims(e::AbstractEmitter)
emitter_ndims(::Type{<:AbstractEmitter})

Return the spatial dimension of emitters, 2, 3, or nothing when there is no single answer.

An emitter's dimension is the one its type declares, and for a type that declares none it is 3 when the emitter has a property z (hasproperty(e, :z)) and 2 when it does not. A collection has its element type's declared dimension, or else the dimension all its elements share, and is nothing when they disagree or when it is empty and its element type declares none (Union{} never does).

SMLMCore's four emitter types declare their dimension, and so does a Union of its types of one dimension; for any other type the type method returns nothing unless the type declares one. Fields are never read: a type that computes z in getproperty is seen through propertynames, as Julia's convention for getproperty asks, and a type that stores z but leaves it out of propertynames is 2D. Emitter types from other packages need nothing else. A type whose propertynames lists a property that getproperty refuses breaks that convention and is not supported.

A type declares its dimension with a method on its type. The declaration then decides for the type, for its elements (whatever their properties) and for empty vectors of it:

SMLMCore.emitter_ndims(::Type{<:MyLocalization{N}}) where {N} = N

A caller that needs a number must handle nothing itself, for example d = emitter_ndims(smld); d === nothing && throw(ArgumentError("emitters are mixed 2D/3D or empty")), or treat an empty input as a no-op before asking. Do not compare the result with < or > without that check.

Example

julia> emitter_ndims(Emitter2DFit{Float64})
2

julia> emitter_ndims(Emitter3D{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 0.1, 500.0))
3

julia> mixed = SMLMCore.AbstractEmitter[Emitter2D{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 500.0),
                                        Emitter3D{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 0.1, 500.0)];

julia> emitter_ndims(mixed) === nothing
true

julia> struct MyEmitter <: SMLMCore.AbstractEmitter   # a type from another package
           x::Float64
           y::Float64
           photons::Float64
       end

julia> emitter_ndims(MyEmitter(1.0, 2.0, 500.0))   # no property z
2

julia> emitter_ndims(MyEmitter) === nothing   # the type declares no dimension
true

Cameras

SMLMCore.AbstractCamera — Type
AbstractCamera

Supertype of all camera types. A camera gives the pixel geometry of the full image.

A concrete camera type has the fields pixel_edges_x and pixel_edges_y: the pixel edges in µm, increasing, N + 1 of them for N pixels, starting at the camera's top-left corner, (0, 0). Pixels need not be uniform. Constructors do not check that the edges increase from 0; physical_to_pixel* and the uniform-pixel formulas assume it, and the cameras' displays assume uniform pixels (they report the first pixel's width). Pixels and coordinates gives the convention.

source
SMLMCore.IdealCamera — Type
IdealCamera{T} <: AbstractCamera

A camera with a regular pixel grid and Poisson noise only, defined by its pixel edges.

Fields

  • pixel_edges_x::Vector{T}: pixel edges in x (µm), from 0, one more than the number of pixels
  • pixel_edges_y::Vector{T}: pixel edges in y (µm), from 0, one more than the number of pixels

Pixel (x, y) = (col, row) is centered at ((col - 0.5)·Δx, (row - 0.5)·Δy) µm, where Δx and Δy are the pixel sizes; see Pixels and coordinates.

Constructors

  • IdealCamera(nx, ny, pixel_size): nx × ny pixels of pixel_size µm at the sample. pixel_size is a number (square pixels) or a tuple (size_x, size_y) of one type (rectangular pixels); T is its type.
  • IdealCamera(pixel_centers_x, pixel_centers_y, pixel_size): the same, with the pixel counts given as AbstractUnitRanges. Only their lengths are used; the edges always start at 0, so 101:612 gives the same camera as 1:512.
  • IdealCamera(pixel_edges_x, pixel_edges_y): from the edges themselves (µm, Vector{T}).

Example

julia> cam = IdealCamera(512, 512, 0.1)
IdealCamera{Float64} with:
  Dimensions: 512 × 512 pixels
  Pixel size: 0.1 μm
  Field of view: 51.2 × 51.2 μm

julia> cam.pixel_edges_x[1:3]
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.1
 0.2

See also

SCMOSCamera, get_pixel_centers

SMLMCore.SCMOSCamera — Type
SCMOSCamera{T<:Real} <: AbstractCamera

An sCMOS camera: the pixel geometry and the calibration of each pixel, in the units of a camera's specification sheet.

Fields

  • pixel_edges_x::Vector{T}, pixel_edges_y::Vector{T}: pixel edges (µm), as for IdealCamera
  • offset::Union{T, Matrix{T}}: dark level (ADU)
  • gain::Union{T, Matrix{T}}: conversion gain (e⁻/ADU)
  • readnoise::Union{T, Matrix{T}}: read noise (e⁻ rms)
  • qe::Union{T, Matrix{T}}: quantum efficiency (0 to 1)

Each calibration field is a scalar, the same for every pixel, or a map of size (ny, nx) indexed [row, col] = [y, x], the same order as the camera image. A square sensor's map cannot be checked for orientation: a transposed map is accepted silently. sCMOS calibration gives the signal chain these parameters describe.

Constructors

  • SCMOSCamera(nx, ny, pixel_size, readnoise; offset=zero(T), gain=one(T), qe=one(T)): nx × ny pixels of pixel_size µm at the sample, a number or a tuple (size_x, size_y).
  • SCMOSCamera(pixel_edges_x, pixel_edges_y; readnoise, offset=zero(T), gain=one(T), qe=one(T)): from pixel edges (µm, Vector{T}), which need not be uniform; readnoise is required.

pixel_size (or the edges), readnoise, offset, gain and qe share one number type T, the type of pixel_size: write offset = 100.0, not 100, and pass maps as Matrix{T}. A call that mixes number types fails: with an UndefVarError (T not defined) when scalars of different types are passed positionally, a MethodError for a map of another element type, and a TypeError for a keyword. A map of the wrong size throws a DimensionMismatch.

Example

A 2048 × 2048 sensor of 6.5 µm pixels behind a 100× objective has pixels of 0.065 µm at the sample:

julia> SCMOSCamera(2048, 2048, 0.065, 1.6; offset = 100.0, gain = 0.46, qe = 0.72)
SCMOSCamera{Float64} with:
  Dimensions: 2048 × 2048 pixels
  Pixel size: 0.065 μm
  Offset: uniform
  Gain: uniform
  Read noise: uniform
  QE: uniform

A read-noise map for a sensor of 512 columns and 256 rows has size (256, 512):

julia> readnoise_map = fill(1.6, 256, 512);

julia> cam = SCMOSCamera(512, 256, 0.1, readnoise_map; offset = 100.0, gain = 0.5);

julia> size(cam.readnoise), cam.offset
((256, 512), 100.0)

IdealCamera

ROI batches

SMLMCore.SingleROI — Type
SingleROI{T}

One region of interest (ROI): a square image cut from a camera frame, with where and when it was cut.

Fields

  • data::Matrix{T}: the ROI pixels as cut from the camera image, indexed [row, col] = [y, x], in the camera's raw units (ADU for an SCMOSCamera)
  • corner::SVector{2,Int32}: camera pixel (x, y) = (column, row) of the ROI's top-left pixel, 1-based
  • frame_idx::Int32: frame number, 1-based

ROI pixel data[row, col] is camera pixel (x, y) = (corner[1] + col - 1, corner[2] + row - 1); see ROIs.

Constructors

  • SingleROI{T}(data, corner, frame_idx): converts its arguments, so corner can be a tuple and frame_idx any integer.
  • SingleROI(data::Matrix{T}, corner::SVector{2,Int32}, frame_idx::Int32)

Example

julia> roi = SingleROI{Float32}(zeros(Float32, 11, 11), (100, 200), 5)
SingleROI{Float32}:
  Size: 11 × 11 pixels
  Corner: (100, 200) = (col, row)
  Frame: 5

SMLMCore.ROIBatch — Type
ROIBatch{T,N,A,C}

A batch of square ROIs from one camera, stored as one array: the form in which ROI finders hand ROIs to fitters (SMLMBoxer to GaussMLE, for example).

Fields

  • data::A: the ROI pixels, an AbstractArray{T,3} of size (roi_size, roi_size, n_rois) indexed [row, col, roi] = [y, x, roi], as cut from the camera image, in the camera's raw units (ADU for an SCMOSCamera, which a fitter converts with the camera's offset and gain)
  • x_corners::Vector{Int32}: camera column of each ROI's top-left pixel, 1-based
  • y_corners::Vector{Int32}: camera row of each ROI's top-left pixel, 1-based
  • frame_indices::Vector{Int32}: frame number of each ROI, 1-based
  • camera::C: the camera of the full image (an AbstractCamera)
  • roi_size::Int: side of each ROI (pixels)

T is the element type of data, N is 3, A is the array type (Array, or a GPU array) and C the camera type.

ROI pixel data[row, col, k] is camera pixel (x, y) = (x_corners[k] + col - 1, y_corners[k] + row - 1). For uniform pixels of size Δx × Δy (µm) its center is at ((x_corners[k] + col - 1.5)·Δx, (y_corners[k] + row - 1.5)·Δy) µm; see ROIs.

Constructors

  • ROIBatch(data, x_corners, y_corners, frame_indices, camera): the corner and frame vectors may hold any integer type and are converted to Int32. ROIs must be square (else an ArgumentError), and each vector needs one entry per ROI (else a DimensionMismatch).
  • ROIBatch(rois::Vector{SingleROI{T}}, camera): from single ROIs of one size; an empty vector gives an empty batch.

Notes

  • batch[k] returns ROI k as a SingleROI, length(batch) is the number of ROIs, and iterating a batch gives its ROIs in order.
  • Adapt.adapt(CuArray, batch) (after using Adapt, CUDA; both must be added to the environment, Pkg.add(["Adapt", "CUDA"])) moves data to the GPU. The corners, frame indices and camera stay on the host. Indexing and iteration need data on the host; use Adapt.adapt(Array, batch_gpu) first.
    using Adapt, CUDA
batch_gpu = Adapt.adapt(CuArray, batch)
batch_host = Adapt.adapt(Array, batch_gpu)   # before batch_host[k] or iterating

Example

julia> camera = IdealCamera(512, 512, 0.1);

julia> data = zeros(Float32, 11, 11, 3);   # 3 ROIs of 11 × 11 pixels, [y, x, roi]

julia> batch = ROIBatch(data, [100, 200, 300], [50, 60, 70], [1, 1, 2], camera);

julia> length(batch), batch.roi_size
(3, 11)

julia> Tuple(batch[3].corner), batch[3].frame_idx
((300, 70), 2)

SingleROI, IdealCamera, SCMOSCamera

SMLD containers

SMLMCore.AbstractSMLD — Type
AbstractSMLD

Supertype of SMLD (single molecule localization data) containers: a set of emitters with the camera they were imaged on. Emitter positions are in µm.

A custom SMLD type subtypes AbstractSMLD and has the fields emitters, camera, n_frames, n_datasets and metadata::Dict{String,Any}. It also needs a constructor MySMLD(emitters, camera, n_frames, n_datasets, metadata) (the default constructor, when the fields are in that order), because the filters, cat_smld and merge_smld build their result with typeof(smld)(emitters, camera, n_frames, n_datasets, metadata). Its emitters meet the requirements in AbstractEmitter.

length(smld) is the number of emitters, and iterating an SMLD iterates its emitters.

SMLMCore.BasicSMLD — Type
BasicSMLD{T,E<:AbstractEmitter} <: AbstractSMLD

The general-purpose SMLD container: emitters of one type, the camera they were imaged on, and the numbers of frames and datasets.

Fields

  • emitters::Vector{E}: the localizations
  • camera::AbstractCamera: the camera of the full image
  • n_frames::Int: number of frames in the acquisition
  • n_datasets::Int: number of datasets
  • metadata::Dict{String,Any}: any other information, by name

T is the element type of the camera's pixel edges (not of the emitters), and E is the emitter type.

Constructors

  • BasicSMLD(emitters, camera, n_frames, n_datasets, metadata = Dict{String,Any}()): metadata must be a Dict{String,Any}; write Dict{String,Any}("exposure_time" => 0.1). A plain Dict("exposure_time" => 0.1) is a Dict{String,Float64} and has no matching method.

Example

julia> cam = IdealCamera(512, 512, 0.1);

julia> emitters = [

           Emitter2DFit{Float64}(5.0, 5.0, 1200.0, 12.0, 0.01, 0.01, 60.0, 2.0; frame=2),
       ];

julia> smld = BasicSMLD(emitters, cam, 2, 1, Dict{String,Any}("exposure_time" => 0.1));

julia> length(smld), smld.n_frames, smld.metadata["exposure_time"]
(2, 2, 0.1)

julia> print(smld)
BasicSMLD(2 2D emitters, 2 frames)

Coordinates

SMLMCore.pixel_to_physical — Function
pixel_to_physical(px, py, pixel_size) -> (x, y)

Convert pixel coordinates to positions in µm, for square pixels of pixel_size µm at the sample with the camera's edges starting at 0.

Pixel coordinates are 1-based: px is the column and py the row, and (1, 1) is the center of the top-left pixel, so x = (px - 0.5) * pixel_size. The result has pixel_size's type, so pixel_size must be a float. For rectangular pixels or a camera with its own edges, use get_pixel_centers. Pixels and coordinates gives the convention.

Example

julia> pixel_to_physical(1, 1, 0.1)   # the center of the top-left pixel
(0.05, 0.05)

julia> pixel_to_physical(10.5, 3, 0.1)
(1.0, 0.25)

See also

physical_to_pixel, physical_to_pixel_index

source
SMLMCore.physical_to_pixel — Function
physical_to_pixel(x, y, pixel_size) -> (px, py)

Convert a position in µm to pixel coordinates, the inverse of pixel_to_physical, for square pixels of pixel_size µm at the sample with the camera's edges starting at 0.

px is the column and py the row, 1-based, and (1, 1) is the center of the top-left pixel, so px = x / pixel_size + 0.5; the camera's corner, (0, 0) µm, is at (0.5, 0.5). The result has the type of x / pixel_size, so Float32 inputs give Float32. For the integer index of the pixel that holds a position, use physical_to_pixel_index.

Example

julia> physical_to_pixel(0.05, 0.25, 0.1)
(1.0, 3.0)
source
SMLMCore.physical_to_pixel_index — Function
physical_to_pixel_index(x, y, pixel_size) -> (px, py)

Return the 1-based (column, row) index of the pixel that contains the position, for square pixels of pixel_size µm with the camera's edges starting at 0.

Along each axis, pixel n covers the half-open interval [(n - 1)·pixel_size, n·pixel_size), up to floating-point rounding: a position computed as (n - 1) * pixel_size can fall in pixel n - 1. A position outside the camera gives an index outside 1:N; the function does not check bounds.

Example

julia> physical_to_pixel_index(0.0, 0.0, 0.1)   # the camera's corner is in pixel (1, 1)
(1, 1)

julia> physical_to_pixel_index(0.25, 0.05, 0.125)   # 0.25 µm is the left edge of pixel 3
(3, 1)

julia> physical_to_pixel_index(0.3, 0.05, 0.1)   # 0.3 / 0.1 rounds to just below 3
(3, 1)

Filtering

SMLMCore.@filter — Macro
@filter(smld, condition)

Return a new SMLD with the emitters for which condition holds, a condition written in terms of emitter properties.

Every name in condition is read as an emitter property, except the operators <, >, <=, >=, ==, !=, in and ∈, and && and ||. A condition therefore holds only property names, number literals and tuples; comparisons may be chained (1.0 <= x <= 5.0). It cannot use a variable (photons > threshold reads e.threshold), a function (abs(x)), arithmetic (x + y), !, a range (frame in 1:10) or the operators ≤ and ≥; each throws an error that names the missing field (a FieldError from Julia 1.12, an ErrorException before). For those, filter the emitters directly:

BasicSMLD(filter(e -> e.photons > threshold, smld.emitters), smld.camera,
          smld.n_frames, smld.n_datasets, copy(smld.metadata))

The result has a new emitter vector, but its emitters are the input's own mutable objects: setting bright.emitters[1].photons = 0 changes smld as well. deepcopy the result before changing emitters in place. The same holds for filter_roi, filter_frames and cat_smld.

Example

julia> cam = IdealCamera(64, 64, 0.1);

julia> smld = BasicSMLD([
           Emitter2DFit{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 1000.0, 10.0, 0.01, 0.01, 50.0, 2.0; frame=1),
           Emitter2DFit{Float64}(3.0, 4.0, 1200.0, 12.0, 0.03, 0.03, 60.0, 2.0; frame=5),
       ], cam, 5, 1);

julia> length(@filter(smld, photons > 1100))
1

julia> length(@filter(smld, σ_x < 0.02 && frame in (1, 2)))
1

julia> length(@filter(smld, 0.5 <= x <= 5.0))
2

SMLMCore.filter_roi — Function
filter_roi(smld, x_range, y_range)
filter_roi(smld, x_range, y_range, z_range)

Return a new SMLD with the emitters inside a rectangle (2D) or a box (3D).

Arguments

  • x_range, y_range, z_range: the bounds in µm, inclusive, each a tuple (min, max) or a range. A range's smallest and largest elements are the bounds, so 0.0:0.3:1.0 stops at 0.9.

Notes

The emitters' dimension (emitter_ndims) picks the form: the 2D form needs dimension 2 and the 3D form dimension 3, for SMLMCore's emitter types and for emitter types from other packages alike. Empty data whose dimension is nothing give an empty result from either form. Otherwise an ErrorException is thrown: "2D ROI cannot be applied to 3D emitter type" (or its 3D counterpart) for data of the other dimension, and "2D ROI cannot be applied to mixed 2D/3D emitters" (or its 3D counterpart) for mixed data. The result's emitters are the input's own objects, as for @filter.

SMLMCore.cat_smld — Function
cat_smld(smlds::Vector{<:AbstractSMLD})
cat_smld(smlds::AbstractSMLD...)

Concatenate SMLDs into one, keeping every emitter's frame and dataset numbers.

Returns

  • A new SMLD of the first input's type that holds every input's emitters, in order.

Notes

  • The cameras must be the same: the same type and equal values in every field, calibration included (a scalar and a uniform map of the same value differ). Otherwise an ErrorException names the input and the fields that differ.
  • All inputs must hold the same emitter type.
  • n_frames and n_datasets are the largest of the inputs'.
  • Metadata is a copy of the first input's, plus "concatenated_from" => number of inputs. The other inputs' metadata are dropped.
  • The result's emitters are the inputs' own objects (see @filter).
  • With one input, the result is a new SMLD with a new emitter vector and the "concatenated_from" key, as with several. An empty vector throws an ErrorException.
  • cat_smld(xs) gives the same emitters, n_frames and n_datasets as merge_smld(xs) without renumbering; only the metadata keys differ.

Example

julia> cam = IdealCamera(64, 64, 0.1);

julia> a = BasicSMLD([Emitter2DFit{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 1000.0, 10.0, 0.01, 0.01, 50.0, 2.0)], cam, 1, 1);

julia> b = BasicSMLD([Emitter2DFit{Float64}(3.0, 4.0, 1200.0, 12.0, 0.01, 0.01, 60.0, 2.0)], cam, 1, 1);

julia> c = cat_smld(a, b);

julia> length(c), c.n_frames, c.metadata["concatenated_from"]
(2, 1, 2)

SMLMCore.merge_smld — Function
merge_smld(smlds::Vector{<:AbstractSMLD}; adjust_frames=false, adjust_datasets=false)
merge_smld(smlds::AbstractSMLD...; adjust_frames=false, adjust_datasets=false)

Merge SMLDs into one, optionally renumbering frames or datasets so that the inputs stay apart.

Keyword Arguments

  • adjust_frames=false: place each input's frames after the earlier inputs' frames. Needs emitters with a frame property.
  • adjust_datasets=false: place each input's datasets after the earlier inputs' datasets. Needs emitters with a dataset property.

Emitter2D and Emitter3D have neither property; renumbering them throws an error that names the missing field (a FieldError from Julia 1.12, an ErrorException before).

Returns

  • A new SMLD of the first input's type that holds every input's emitters, in order.

Notes

  • The cameras must be the same, by the rule in cat_smld, and all inputs must hold the same emitter type.
  • With adjust_frames, each input's frame numbers move by the sum of the earlier inputs' n_frames, and the result's n_frames is that sum over all inputs; without it, n_frames is the largest of the inputs'. adjust_datasets does the same for dataset numbers and n_datasets. An input whose emitters lie in datasets 1:n_datasets therefore keeps its datasets apart from every other input's; numbers an input declares but does not use stay empty in the result.
  • The inputs are never modified. Each renumbered emitter is a new object, one per entry, even when an input lists the same emitter object more than once. Only the requested numbers are read and changed, so an emitter type with a frame field and no dataset field can be renumbered by frame, and immutable emitter types can be renumbered. Fields of a renumbered emitter that hold arrays or other objects stay shared with the input emitter. The first input's emitters (and, without renumbering, every input's) are the inputs' own objects, as in cat_smld.
  • Metadata is a copy of the first input's, plus "merged_from" (the number of inputs), "frame_adjustment" and "dataset_adjustment" (the two keywords). The other inputs' metadata are dropped.
  • With one input, the result is a new SMLD with these metadata keys, as with several; its numbers do not move. An empty vector throws an ErrorException.

Example

julia> cam = IdealCamera(64, 64, 0.1);

julia> a = BasicSMLD([Emitter2DFit{Float64}(1.0, 2.0, 1000.0, 10.0, 0.01, 0.01, 50.0, 2.0; frame=3)], cam, 10, 1);

julia> b = BasicSMLD([Emitter2DFit{Float64}(3.0, 4.0, 1200.0, 12.0, 0.01, 0.01, 60.0, 2.0; frame=3)], cam, 10, 1);

julia> m = merge_smld(a, b; adjust_frames = true);

julia> [e.frame for e in m.emitters], m.n_frames
([3, 13], 20)

julia> b.emitters[1].frame   # the input is unchanged
3
